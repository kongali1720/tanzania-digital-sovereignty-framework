# Business & Investment Framework

> **Status:** Proposed Commercial & Investment Framework (Draft for Institutional Review)  
> **Scope:** Financing Models, PPP/BOT Delivery Structures, Economic Sustainability, and Governance Guardrails for the Tanzania Digital Sovereignty Framework.

---

## 1. Cost Structure & Phased Investment Approach

Sovereign Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI), Zero Trust security, and permissioned integrity layers require disciplined capital allocation over multi-year horizons:

* **Capital Expenditure (CapEx):** Core identity/exchange software stacks, Hardware Security Modules (HSMs), sovereign data center/compute capacity, initial PKI setup, and baseline security tooling.
* **Operational Expenditure (OpEx):** Continuous 24/7 monitoring (SIEM/SOAR), validator/node operations, certificate/HSM maintenance, software patching, bandwidth, and skilled engineering staffing.
* **Phased Milestone Funding:** Release funding across bounded phases (Foundational DPI & IAM -> Interoperability & Audit Layer -> Expanded Ecosystem Services) gated by verifiable technical, security, and operational readiness criteria rather than upfront all-at-once commitments.

---

## 2. Financing & Delivery Models (PPP, BOT & Hybrid)

| Model | Best Fit Use Case | Sovereign & Risk Allocation Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Public / Sovereign-Funded Delivery** | Core identity/trust roots, PKI/HSM key control, primary regulatory/audit registries | Highest sovereign control; requires predictable budget appropriation and internal technical capacity. |
| **Public–Private Partnership (PPP)** | Shared portals, ecosystem service integration, managed cloud/connectivity infrastructure | Allocates operational/service delivery risk to private partners while retaining policy, data ownership, and oversight with public/sovereign authorities. |
| **Build–Operate–Transfer (BOT)** | Specialized infrastructure, data center/node operations, or platform buildouts with defined transition | Enables faster initial build and operational ramp-up, followed by structured knowledge transfer, asset handover, and exit readiness. |
| **Grants & Technical Cooperation** | Standards research, pilot sandboxes, capacity building, interoperability studies | Useful for non-core enablement and technical exchange; must not compromise data sovereignty, key control, or procurement independence. |

---

## 3. Economic Sustainability & Value Drivers

Long-term sustainability depends on recurring operational value rather than one-off pilot launches:

* **Shared Infrastructure Reuse:** Reusing common identity, API gateway, schema validation, and audit services reduces redundant per-agency integration costs.
* **Service Efficiency & Transaction Scale:** Standardized digital exchanges lower manual processing overhead, reconciliation friction, and service delivery turnaround times.
* **SLA, Lifecycle & Exit Readiness:** Tie vendor/operator compensation to measurable uptime, security posture, and incident response SLAs; mandate open formats, documented APIs, and source/data escrow or handover clauses to prevent lock-in.

---

## 4. Governance & Transparency Guardrails

* **Sovereignty Non-Negotiables:** Private or external delivery partners under PPP/BOT arrangements must not gain unilateral control over sovereign root keys, core registries, or unrestricted access to sensitive citizen/institutional data.
* **Clear Fact vs. Proposal Boundaries:** Financing structures, PPP/BOT options, and cost/value mechanisms in this document are **proposed models for evaluation**. Active funding, procurement awards, or institutional partnerships are only stated as confirmed when backed by verified evidence.
